FILE: vqf.pdf
CITATION: Laidig D., Seel T. — "VQF: Highly Accurate IMU Orientation Estimation
          with Bias Estimation and Magnetic Disturbance Rejection"
          Information Fusion 91 (2023) 187–204
          arXiv: 2203.17024
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The VQF algorithm is the direct baseline of the new method. Key characteristics:
- 9DOF IMU orientation estimation (gyroscope + accelerometer + magnetometer)
- Accelerometer low-pass filter applied in an "almost-inertial frame"
  (near-earth/inertial frame, NOT the gyroscope/body frame)
- Maintains three separate quaternion states: gyro-only, acc+gyro, acc+gyro+mag
  — for 9DOF output all three correction stages must be computed sequentially
  — this is the main computational cost the new paper targets
- Includes gyroscope bias estimation and magnetic disturbance rejection
- Evaluated with a single fixed parametrization across a large diverse dataset
  collection (broad generalization without parameter tuning)
- Compared against 8 literature methods; achieved RMSE 2.9° vs 5.3°–16.7°
  for competing methods
- Open-source implementation provided

Relevance to new paper:
- The acc low-pass filtering in the near-earth/inertial frame is adopted from VQF
  (this is the "good approach from VQF" noted in the algorithm description)
- VQF exposes three separate quaternion states (gyro / acc+gyro / acc+gyro+mag);
  the new method collapses this to two states (gyro / gyro+acc+mag), where acc
  corrects roll+pitch and mag corrects yaw within one combined correction step
- This reduction from three sequential state corrections to one is the primary
  source of the ~40% speedup
- Informationally, acc and mag still correct separate axes in the new method —
  the gain is structural (fewer state updates), not algorithmic fusion of axes
- VQF is the primary speed and accuracy comparison baseline
- Accuracy trade-off: few percent higher error vs VQF for undisturbed motion

FILE: Justa_sensors-20-03824.pdf
CITATION: Justa J., Šmídl V., Hamáček A. — "Fast AHRS Filter for Accelerometer,
          Magnetometer, and Gyroscope Combination with Separated Sensor Corrections"
          Sensors 20(14):3824, MDPI, 2020
          DOI: 10.3390/s20143824
          48 citations
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The author's previous paper introducing the Justa AHRS filter. Key contributions:
- Predictor-corrector framework: prediction from gyroscopes + independent
  correction steps for accelerometer and magnetometer separately
- Two filter variants:
    (a) SO(3)-based operations — maximum accuracy
    (b) Linearized operations — faster computation
- Both variants outperformed state-of-the-art in accuracy while requiring less
  compute
- Introduced the magnetometer correction step formulation that is carried into
  the new paper
- Validated with low-cost MEMS sensors and VICON optical tracking ground truth
- Open datasets and source code published

Relevance to new paper:
- The magnetometer correction step formulation is inherited from this work
- In both Justa AHRS and the new method, acc and mag corrections are
  informationally separated: acc corrects roll/pitch, mag corrects yaw —
  but they are applied in a single combined correction step (one state update)
- Must be cited as the origin of the combined single-step correction
- Positions the current paper as an optimization building on Justa AHRS +
  VQF earth-frame filtering concepts
